# Desktop subscriptions and account services

INPUT: A verified Supabase account, explicitly configured Stripe account/environment and RevenueCat Stripe app, and server UTC.
OUTPUT: One account trial, transaction-bound paid facts, service eligibility, recoverable billing operations, retained-data access and bounded deletion processing.
POS: Authoritative billing, desktop purchase and cloud-admission contract. D0–D3 development engineering and provider/native acceptance are complete. Production activation and release verification remain separate decisions.

## Scope and fixed policy

Plus provides encrypted sync and built-in AI. Local work and BYOK do not require billing. Prices are USD 899 cents/month and 7900 cents/year, inclusive of applicable tax. There are no discounts, provider trials or automatic overage charges. Stripe Tax requires actual registrations before automatic tax is enabled. AI protections and the existing shared budget remain independent and unchanged.

An explicit trial claim starts exactly 192 hours at the authoritative database clock. The account/environment can claim only once. A confirmed paid window consumes an existing trial atomically with paid access. Financial success blocks a new grant, but pending RevenueCat recognition preserves an already active trial at its original deadline. A first reconciliation that discovers a verified refunded/disputed historical payment permanently consumes eligibility. Refunds do not restore it.

Cancellation preserves the current paid window. Both cycle changes use the same subscription and a future schedule phase, without immediate proration. The next phase starts at the current paid-period end and sets `billing_cycle_anchor: 'phase_start'`, so the new cycle is billed from that boundary. The current phase keeps its existing price and dates. Cancellation releases the schedule before setting period-end cancellation. A paid-to-paid automatic renewal failure has at most 72 hours from its original renewal boundary; retries do not extend that deadline. First purchases, ordinary cancellation, trial expiry, refunds and disputes do not receive grace.

RevenueCat is the purchase aggregator. Stripe is used to establish object ownership, verify paid invoices and compensate for refunds/disputes (including older invoices). Each invoice is a separate fact: a refund/dispute cannot revoke a later unaffected invoice. Current full refunds and disputes stop future renewal. Partial refunds do not shorten service. A won/withdrawn dispute can restore a still-valid transaction, without restoring trial or automatically resuming renewal.

RevenueCat recognition matches the configured price and exact invoice period. Besides its current period, a subscription may expose an already-paid next period while its current-period fields still follow real time. That candidate requires `auto_renewal_status=has_already_renewed`, an active next period with access and no pending payment, the same verified owner/store/environment/subscription item, a verified product from the configured app, and an end matching `ends_at`. Omitted next-period fields inherit the current value; explicit null does not. A scheduled product change or failed upcoming renewal alone cannot confirm payment. Stripe must independently report the original invoice as paid. SQL still requires the paid period to have started before granting access.

### D3 development worker contract

The existing `lifecycle_identity.p_owner` can identify an Auth owner or an internal subject. ### D1 implementation contract

Cloud transports send `x-goalloom-service-version: 1`; this is compatibility metadata and does not replace authorization or change cryptographic protocol 1. PostgREST uses `PT409`, `PT403` and `PT503` for the finite upgrade, service and configuration codes above. Clients classify service errors before authentication failures and preserve account identity, keys, pending writes and receipt hashes. Exact receipt recovery accepts parallel original operation IDs and payload hashes and remains reachable before ordinary sync status. Realtime uses `cloud:v1:ws:<workspaceId>` with server-only publication and eligibility checked at notification emission as well as data admission. Snapshot transfer requires authenticated bounded access; mint-time checks alone do not revoke previously issued Storage credentials. Legacy credentials and connections require an observed drain boundary.

Every public billing status result includes the server environment and a validated `priceCatalog` with monthly/annual amounts in integer minor units, `USD` currency and `inclusive` tax behavior. Subscription records add `cycle` and `nextCycle` from the exact configured prices. Unknown prices require review rather than a guessed plan. Existing trusted period-end and cancellation fields control display and future changes.

An authenticated operation failure includes a closed-operation receipt only after its original request/action/cycle and terminal result are durably stored. The client checks the environment, current account revision and original or previously bound canonical operation before removing its local pending entry. A timeout, generic conflict or error code alone does not establish closure. Background status refresh does not complete an open Portal visit, and a callback during another request queues one account-bound reconciliation.

Completed operation replays return fresh authoritative status, including historical receipts outside the recent-operation lookup window. A completed Checkout includes its immutable request/action/cycle receipt; matching that receipt can finish the local journal even when current service is pending, expired or revoked. Completion never grants eligibility. Renderer mutations carry their initiating account ID only as an IPC stale-view guard; main rejects an account mismatch before writing a journal or sending a request. The backend still obtains identity solely from verified Auth.

Cross-device Checkout reuse persists an immutable caller-to-canonical operation binding before responding. Later requests resolve that binding before newer pending work, including after the canonical operation has ended. Terminal alias receipts identify both the original caller as `requestedId` and the canonical operation as `requestId`; this lets a caller recover even if its first alias response was lost. Clients must match the original intent and reject a changed known canonical ID. An incompatible new intent receives its own persisted closed receipt rather than becoming an unexpected purchase when retried later.

Checkout completes locally only from its matching terminal receipt, never from an unrelated active subscription projection. Explicit status checks and accepted callbacks may include the original finite Checkout intent in reconciliation. The backend then resolves its operation/alias read-only: an absent or pending operation adds no terminal receipt, and no operation, continuation, alias or provider session is created. Status checking cannot reopen the browser. Background GET updates eligibility without consuming Checkout or Portal intents. Bare and receipt-bound reconciliation both return the authoritative status after all completed/closed Checkout transitions.

External browser URLs are constrained to known HTTPS Stripe hosts and finite session paths. Portal supports both the legacy `/p/session/<token>` path and the observed `/p/session?secret=<opaque>` shape with exactly one bounded secret parameter; credentials, fragments, extra/duplicate parameters and non-default ports are rejected. Browser session secrets never enter logs or public artifacts.

For first-run users who chose account login and have verified trial/paid access, offer explicit sync setup or local setup before creating a local calendar. Sync setup reuses the existing password, merge and recovery flow; login itself does not discover/register a workspace. Local setup still requires calendar confirmation and then skips optional AI. An already configured workspace does not receive this extra first-run choice. Once calendar setup has begun, background eligibility refresh or expiry cannot unmount it or discard drafts; service qualification and the current setup stage remain separate.

Checkout and Portal accept a finite continuation containing version 1, a UUID, one of the five shipped locales, and transport `app` or `loopback`. Production permits only the app transport. Development requires a loopback port from 1024 through 65535. The backend immutably binds this object to the verified account, environment and original operation before provider creation. A retry cannot alter it. Continuations expire within 24 hours of the original receipt; provider session validity remains independently authoritative.

The backend constructs only exact localized `/billing/continue/` and `/billing/return/` routes at `https://www.goalloom.com`. Query fields are limited to `v`, `environment`, `continue`, `transport` and, for development, `port`. They carry neither authentication nor billing authority. Website controls require an explicit click and reconstruct only `goalloom://billing-return` in production or `http://127.0.0.1:<port>/billing-return` in development, with version, environment and continuation UUID. The website does not authenticate, query account state, declare payment success or fetch a loopback address. Invalid input is removed and falls back to manual app instructions.

Main accepts a callback only against its current account/environment and unexpired local intent, then reconciles the original operation under existing Auth. A return never starts a trial, creates a purchase or grants service. Restart reuses the original loopback port where available; port conflicts preserve manual status recovery. Recovering another device's same-account checkout preserves that session and its original continuation. Responses identify the canonical operation and whether it can return to this device; the client stores the canonical reference separately and offers manual checking when needed. An offline or lost callback never discards the unresolved operation.

Retrieval v1 uses authenticated owner + generation + fixed `headSeq`/roster/checkpoint identity + verified recovery-key proof. D2 source and staged database acceptance do not imply hosted deployment. Password-derived keys stay local. New-device retrieval receives a read-only capability bound to that boundary and necessary envelopes/control metadata; it never receives upload, roster mutation or continuous pull permission. Expiry pins `accessEndedAt`, `retrieveUntil = accessEndedAt + 720h`, `onlineDeleteBy = retrieveUntil + 168h`, lifecycle version and cleanup state (`retained`, `queued`, `deleting`, `deleted`, `explicit_delete`). Revocation starts from verified server enforcement time, not an old purchase timestamp. D2 persists and fences these records. Only qualification restored before the cutoff (or verified to have existed before it) cancels ordinary cleanup for that generation. Later purchases require a new generation. Explicit deletion wins over callbacks; old generations never revive. Service backups, if retained, follow the applicable provider policies and actual configuration, which must be disclosed. They are isolated and unavailable for user recovery. Infrastructure restoration must replay deletions before access reopens. The owner withdrew the numeric supplier-backup expiry commitment on 2026-10-25; the initial approach retains Supabase Free and existing local backups, with no new self-hosted or paid backup service; Pro may be considered only after real paying users, without automatic purchase. Pro/new backups are not initial-launch prerequisites. Post-expiry retrieval remains pending discussion, with the current 720-hour rule unchanged. No daily backup SLA or paid backup service is approved. Billing facts retain only necessary financial metadata for seven calendar years after the last relevant financial event; post-deletion trial protection is keyed and bounded to 180 days.

Retrieval bootstrap v1 is independent of device registration. During the retained interval, an owner with a valid Auth session but no registered device may request only the retained recovery descriptor (KDF algorithm/parameters/salt, recovery-key identifier and public verification key, encrypted recovery envelope), workspace generation, trusted checkpoint/head hash and signed roster anchor, plus a random, single-use challenge with a server expiry. The server resolves ownership and generation itself; it returns no task ciphertext before proof. The client derives password keys locally, decrypts the existing recovery key and signs a domain-separated retrieval proof over the challenge, Auth session identifier, generation, checkpoint/roster/head hashes, requested fixed boundary, cutoff and purpose. Passwords, derived keys and recovery private keys never leave the device. Public verification material must chain to the retained trusted checkpoint/roster anchor; client-provided keys cannot replace it.

After verifying the proof and current retention state atomically, the server issues a short-lived read-only capability bound to the owner Auth session, generation, fixed `headSeq` and signed chain/checkpoint/roster anchor, `retrieveUntil`, nonce, purpose and expiry (no later than the cutoff). It permits only that snapshot's ciphertext and necessary envelopes/control metadata. It grants no device registration, roster write, recovery rotation, upload or continuous pull. Neither `register_device` nor `complete_recovery` is called, and retrieval writes no roster entry. Challenge/capability bookkeeping is separate from workspace data. Every read rechecks session validity, generation/lifecycle fence, capability scope and cutoff. Challenges are consumed once; a lost response requires a new challenge, and stale/replayed/cross-session proofs fail closed. Capability reads may retry only within the same immutable boundary and validity window.

Invalid Auth returns 401; unsupported protocol returns `upgrade_required`; invalid proof, scope or replay returns `retrieval_proof_invalid`; changed generation/head trust anchor returns `retrieval_boundary_changed` and requires a new bootstrap; expired challenge/capability returns `retrieval_capability_expired` while the retained interval remains open. At or after `retrieveUntil`, or after explicit deletion/deleting/deleted state, bootstrap, issuance and reads return `retrieval_closed` without disclosing retained metadata. Concurrent cleanup/qualification changes are checked under the same lifecycle fence; no challenge or previously issued capability extends retention or restores an old generation. New-device acceptance must exercise this independent owner-authenticated bootstrap; existing session-bound `get_roster`/`get_key_envelopes` and mutating recovery flows cannot satisfy it.

Old clients receive `upgrade_required` and pause cloud work without clearing local outbox, original receipt IDs/hashes, keys or cursors. They must not retry device registration or mutating recovery as a retrieval fallback. Retrieval installs an inspectable separate copy by default; replacement requires explicit choice and a protective local backup. No executable retrieval endpoint exists in D0.

### D2 account lifecycle and data contract

Migrations `0025`–`0028` introduce a random internal subject with a nullable live Auth mapping and forward re-key the existing billing and AI facts. Provider customer identities, transaction facts, unknown outcomes, compensation keys and cost totals survive that change. Only verified existing Auth can create a live mapping; provider callbacks and workers cannot recreate it after deletion. The original billing coordinator processes closed subjects through a settlement-only path. A late active subscription preserves financial facts and keeps renewal pending until cancellation is verified; it cannot restore cloud access.

The shared lock order is the optional AI budget lock, an identity-creation lock when required, the subject/account fence, sorted workspace fences, then child rows. Network calls run outside those database locks. A late payment qualifies for ordinary recovery only from its verified payment/qualification time, not merely the invoice period start. An explicit deletion remains irreversible even if later payment facts arrive.

Deletion uses a SQL outbox and an independently durable ledger with append/get/head/read. The worker must verify the original exported intent before destructive dispatch. It inventories only the closed workspace/generation namespace, checks object absence and rescans before marking content deleted. Auth deletion retains its exact original target across a lost response and FK removal. Renewal failures do not block content/Auth progress, and bounded leases must not starve later accounts. Unknown upload outcomes remain pending unless the original producer and Storage effect bounds are independently proved; an Abort signal alone is insufficient. Read-only downloads cannot prevent deletion. Production requires an independent ledger, externally enforced restore quarantine, reliable scheduling and actual provider-backup policy disclosure with restore-safety verification. The local SQLite adapter proves development behavior only.

Deletion preparation expires 24 hours after its original creation. Main protects a random 32-byte status secret before preparation; the backend stores only its SHA-256 digest. Confirmation pins the request and its status receipt expiry at 720 hours from first acceptance. The no-Auth receipt endpoint exposes finite progress only. It cannot initiate deletion, enumerate data or extend expiry. The client retains its original protected receipt until content/Auth and renewal processing are terminal and acknowledged, or until the original expiry. Uncertain confirmation results reuse the same receipt; only definitive nonacceptance permits a new preparation.

Restore replay remains quarantined while it captures a contiguous, fixed ledger prefix and reinstates original account/generation fences. Every resume, including a completed run, rechecks environment, store, key, cursor and actual admission barriers. Replay never releases traffic or restarts deletion deadlines. A missing required original key or incomplete prefix leaves admission blocked. Business-database restoration is not evidence that an independent deletion ledger or a supplier backup has been erased.

The supported retrieval profile is a complete format-1, epoch-1 log with `minRetainedSeq = 0`, checkpoint 1, unchanged recovery public keys and a contiguous verified roster chain. Validate actual operations and receipts, including rows outside the claimed interval. Unsupported or incomplete history returns `retrieval_data_unavailable` without altering originals. A response is at most 4,194,304 UTF-8 bytes, with at most 200 operations or 64 rosters; never skip a row to fit. Challenges expire after at most 300 seconds and capabilities after at most 900 seconds, both capped by the original retrieval cutoff. They bind the verified Auth session, lifecycle revision, exact generation and fixed trust boundary. All four lifecycle readiness flags are required; missing configuration fails closed.

D2 sync admission uses service header 2. Hosted AI retains its separate header 1; encrypted log, retrieval/lifecycle JSON and billing DTOs remain version 1. There is no automatic downgrade. New cloud initialization uses the existing encrypted `seed.begin` / `seed.rows` / `seed.final` operations and shared applier, preserving exact business rows, versions, timestamps, tombstones and guidance. The preflight limits canonical source rows to 100 MiB and each row operation to 200 records and 1,048,536 plaintext bytes. Refusal occurs before retiring the old binding. Local transition request, remote initialization request and encrypted seed identity are distinct immutable values.

Only the original creator may write during pending initialization. Finish verifies the original final operation ID/hash/sequence and current qualification. A matching completed replay returns its immutable receipt and current status, not an access grant. Peers wait for completion. A retained complete seed may be copied independently when final acknowledgement was lost; copying does not finish the live binding. Protected old binding context and retired operational evidence preserve unknown-send recovery. Optional replacement is blocked by current pending/unknown writes and rechecked under maintenance; a separate recovered copy remains available.

A deleted generation always returns `initialization: { state: 'not_applicable' }` without an initialization ID, even when its initialization was purged, was not required or has a stale row. All other status fields remain unchanged. A non-deleted generation with a missing required initialization still returns `lifecycle_unavailable`.

First-run password recovery completes explicit calendar setup and any initial direction write before presenting new-cloud confirmation. Only an account- and local-generation-bound UI intent crosses that setup boundary; no password, preview token or acknowledgement does. The existing recovery dialog then reads the current source, starts with an empty password and unchecked confirmation, and creates the cloud workspace only after that confirmation. Cancellation preserves completed local setup. An account or generation change clears the intent. Qualified accounts continue to skip the optional AI step.

Local password-vault and binding-journal custody is partitioned by the canonical environment, project reference and account ID. File paths, authenticated encryption and decoded records bind the same immutable scope. Password and retained-context records contain only that scope's journal key and allowed device/workspace/recovery state; they never copy a global key map or another account's custody. Persist and verify original key custody before writing a journal or promoting a binding. A missing key with existing protected evidence fails closed and preserves the original bytes. An explicit password operation also prepares recoverable custody when the native vault is available. Automatic updates require the original matching key and lock. This isolates accounts/projects/environments; it does not claim cryptographic erasure of earlier generations within the same account. The independent deletion receipt survives local sync clearing.

Authenticated key rotation advances only the epoch of the same complete binding. Main first persists and reads back the protected original request, verified roster and new-key context. Core atomically compares the full previous binding and runtime epoch, advances both, and records an immutable receipt. On an unknown result, reuse that request and read its receipt; promote custody, journal and transport only after exact receipt and active-identity checks. Missing preparation fails closed. A committed lineage proves the unchanged initialization receipt across later epochs; initialization, task history, pending operations and cursors are not rewritten. A stale write cannot reactivate an earlier epoch. Desktop schema 14 installs the epoch-receipt table and strips its private identity metadata from portable copies.

Portable backups contain no account/device binding tokens or protected-context references. Non-executable guidance checkpoints record exact before/after heads and logical identities at complete local-operation boundaries. Whole-sync installation, absorption and receipt rebuilding capture them atomically. The existing system baseline records an explicit local observation of otherwise unrepresented business state; it does not invent source-device user actions. Strict import validates every local history segment and the final state. Existing operations, events and undo markers remain unchanged; facts across an unproved remote-history gap remain unknown or partial.

Notices are in-app only: service ended, seven days or less remain, retrieval cutoff reached, and confirmed online deletion. Show only the latest applicable stage, use authoritative dates, preserve editing/focus and retain dates in Settings. Hide cloud notice cards while Settings is open so they cannot cover its controls; Settings retains the authoritative dates and actions. No email, push or marketing service is part of D2.

Dismissal is local to the device, account, cloud generation and stage; refresh or restart cannot repeat that same dismissed stage. A later stage may appear. Restored qualification removes obsolete notices for an open generation, while a closed generation retains its correct state even after a new purchase. An account with no cloud copy receives no cloud-deletion notice. A deletion notice requires the authoritative online-deletion timestamp. Notices contain only service state, trusted dates and relevant actions, without task content, workspace names or payment identifiers; they neither grant service access nor trigger cleanup.
